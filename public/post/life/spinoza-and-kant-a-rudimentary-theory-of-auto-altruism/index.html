Problem of Human Condition and Greedy Approach

Dispropagaiton of Local Optima to Global Optima

We want visible results, and tangible reward - and immediate ones at that. We value the present more than the future. Perfectly rational, because who knows you will be there tomorrow right?

In programming, there’s a technique called “greedy approach.” This is a simpleton way of the strategy of making optimal choices at each given condition and state. The idea is, that by choosing optimal result at each step, we will end up with the optimal result at the end.

The problem is, that this is not necessarily true. The critical problem in greedy approaches and why it can’t be used everywhere in programming, is because there are cases where making optimal choices at every step can lead to suboptimal result in the end.

As you can see, if the problem structure does not allows it, greedy approach does not always work. We programmers call this “dispropagation of local optima to global optima due to lack of matroid structure or monotonicity in the problem space and its system of variables.” Basically this refers to the cases in problem solving when maximizing utility, optimizing the return and rewards in the given moment does not result in optimal result in the end (or even desirable for that matter).

So the paradox of this moral equivalent of “greedy approach” is actually easily spottable in our everyday life. You spending hours scrolling on instagram and watching Youtube and Tiktok videos (or whatever you kids do nowadays), is a prime example of this. Note that it is rational for you to watch the Youtube video and scroll through instagram instead of going to the gym, studying for that test, learning that skill, or doing anything you know you should do. It is rational because by not doing those, you are maximizing your utility at the given moment. Doing what you should has immediate cost of pain and exerted effort, but the reward is delayed.

Disconnect of Self

Have you ever noticed a common theme in these things - workout, study, reading, honing skills - all the things that we know we should do but don’t do? The common denominator of these, is that you never enjoy the fruit of your labor. You never get to enjoy the results that these “should do” tasks generate.

Sure, you in the future will be better off. But “you,” the one exerting all the effort, will never enjoy the return on investment. There’s a subtle but inherent disconnect between you who do the work, and you who enjoy the fruits of the labor.

More Accurate Model of Effort and Result: Continuous Result and Discrete Effort

This is a model of effort and result that I want to propose, which I believe more accurately describe the nature of exerted efforts and the results. I will write in more details about this topic in the future, so here is the gist in bullet points.

  • Results are continuous. You don’t get to the next step, next self after x amount of exerted effort. Each unit of effort moves you y amount in the result scale (with some constant multiplier on the efficiency and effectiveness of that effort.)
  • Effort is discrete. What you do today is that and that only: just what you did today. Just because you’ve ran 60 minutes today, doesn’t mean you can skip out tomorrow. You may do more or less on a day, but the effort is reset every base interval unit (typically a day)
  • Result is what you become. Effort is what you do. The fact is, you are nothing but what you’ve done today - and by the extension become tomorrow. The effort you exert on a given day is transformed into partial results, and becomes you. You get to “respawn” in the new self you build out today, and play from that point on.

[Show how result as step function is wrong - you just don’t see the unit of partial result that accumulates so you assume it is 0, thereby interpreting it as step function and binary state of achiving and not achiving. Cumilative effort is wrong. You working hard today is only relevant to today. You have to put in the effort tomorrow again, as if it resets everyday. Note that your effort is turned into partial result that is the new you. The truth is, things didn’t get easier - you got better. This is why you need to see effort as discrete factor and result as continuous one.]
