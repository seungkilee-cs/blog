The term "developer" now extends to web devs, which roughly accounts for 80% of the "developers" out there. But due to this liberality of the extent of this term's umbrella, when you see a lot of complaints, problems, and contemporary dislike of the developers, you realize it is largely problem with these web devs.

It is my personal belief that a web developer is a developer sure, but not necessarily a software engineer. And while some see the distinction here as meaningless and pedantic, I believe this distinction is not only justified but warranted.

## Frameworker vs Problem Solver

Engineers are problem solvers. They define the problem scope and statement, see various ways to frame the problem, and come up with multiple approaches to solve it, and given the constraint choose the right tools and approach.

Web Developers, at large, are frameworkers. They have a digital equivalent of fancy lego - their frameworks. And they put it together. That's it. This is essentially the "pancake batter with eggs" of digital era. pieceing together frameworks and libraries are just difficult enough that not all people can do it, and therefore it is an effective Habitus for their differentiation as Bourdieu says. They can enjoy their relative feelings of superiority, but they don't solve problem. For them, the framework isn't a tool but the entire means of making something.

Software Engineers are people who solve problems with software as a tool. I sincerely believe that terms like "software consultant" or "software engineer" should be eliminated in the future. Because an engineer should seek a software solution to a problem if it is applicable and useful, but one should not only try to solve problems though software.

## differentiating between lack of experience and lack of thought

Lack of experience exhibits itself in inefficiency that is correctable with more context. It is like choosing to use a spoon to eat a bowl of noodles versus a fork or a chopstick. Sure it can work, but it is inefficient given the problem. Usually this stems from lack of exposure to knowledge and experiences. They know how to use a spoon (usually software equivalent of javascript), and believe that spoon can be used to do anything. Therefore they resort to it for every problem. For this case, simply showing them and explaining to them why for the given problem there are more desirable alternatives, can lead them to a different path.

Lack of thoughts is different. It exhibits in lack of desire to learn, try, and expand their knowledge. This is more like a person eating a bowl of noodle through their nose instead of mouth. I honestly do not know what to say to that. I don't know how and where to start because I don't know why you would do that, other than that was the way you learnt how and you never once questioned this. It's not the experience that is lacking, it is the thought. This is the biggest issue I have with a bulk of web developers. They know one way to do things, and do not consider the necessity of any other way until they are forced into it. This is precisely the reason why JAVA Spring Boot MVC is still so widely used in legacy applications, this is why there are bunch of web apps that are wrapped in React and Vue when it could've been a single html file with a few lines of scripts.
