Human condition in the modern days has bias for putting greater cognitive and preferential weight on something that is subjective, which was dominant in the days of the linear media. (ie, queen, beatles, elvis)

This is fetishization of the phenomena, not the media itself. They do not miss the music because it was objectively or collectively subjectively better. They miss the time they were in when those musics were common. This is true for any culture (kpop and anime fans are nostalgic about their childhood anime or band)

But the true weight that people place on this grand normativity of something that is phenomena, is the shared experiences. It was something that anyone and everyone would know. You could meet anyone at a bus stop, cafe, whatever - and if you knew nothing about that person, you could start a conversation about the song or the artist. This grand normativity that came from the monopoly of the linear traditional media, was at its essence a sense of belonging. Knowing that you and I, would share something, and if we didn't I wouldn't be the weird one for knowing it (the sense of safety, for instance, lacks in the anime fan base because they are externalized as weebs and does not ancitipate a person they meet in their day to day life to know about the same context that they immerse themselves in.) We miss that. We miss knowing that there's something common between you and I, regardless of who we are. We miss being us. We miss us.
