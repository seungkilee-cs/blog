Current left agendas are overridden with culture and identity, that it ignores the economics

How did this happen? Why did the left deviate away from Marxism and Materialism? (This is again, why cultural Marxism is a bs term. Left doesn't care about Marx anymore. They are still the children of Marcuse and the 68)

The answer is Taylorism and unprecedented growth post war. This was the only occasion where "wage led growth" was shown to be working. The growth of the 60s have masked class dynamic - incoming water floats all boats, as they say. Proletariats had their boats floating, they were fed and warm. They did not need or want a revolution.

So the left have branched out and expanded their problem space, and redefined their lens in which they observe the world and the problem. It was no longer class, it was culture. Identity. The intangible, immeasurable, ever expanding and infinitely morphing beast that perpetuates its totalitarian victimhood.

The problem is, that if something was flourishing as a result of prosperity, it can then meet walls when that prosperity ends.

Just because a movement incidentally and consequentially derived a desirable and morally good outcome, it cannot mean that they were for equality. This is a pedantic but also important distinction: woman's rights movement was not "necessarily" moral, it incidentally and consequentially were so. Because women were oppressed and unequally treated (like voting rights, economic status) their efforts to improve female rights incidentally led to equality. The problem is, that we too are human. We make a false transitivity: because there's a little, there must be more - because a little is good, more must be better.

Current women's rights movement do not ground their claims in measurable and targetable metrics. Their goal is no longer discrete and visible (like voting rights for women) but they are morally coersive and arbitrarily so.

Now, to why cultural divides, while must be acknowledged, are not the primary metric the policies should target.

1. Economic metrics are measurable, targetable, and discrete. It has sunset clause and workable steps, and can be expanded and iterated upon with modified concensus. This prohibits perpetual and totalitarian victimhood.
2. Culture does not get changed by policy. "Unorderables" will prevail. When you talk about refining and defining culture and identity through policy, I argue that is dangerous 1 because it requires severe limits in personal freedoms and the effects are not measurable. But also 2, even if it is effective, why would democracy want to give that power and precedents to institutionalized power?
3. Economic metrics are less about the intent and morality of someone, and less close to heart for people. But, their impact is much greater and effective. What we should target with policy should be either infringement upon someone else's basic freedom or strictly measurable outcomes.
4. Economic metrics do not require epistemic violence and dogmatism to enforce. When someone is measurably poorer, it doesn't require mental gymnastics of "sure, that black female student majoring in poli sci is technically richer than that working class boy from a redneck household - but he's a white man."
5. Economic metrics are universal whereas culture and identity are fragmented and contexted. Yang Wenri's famous saying "It is morally better to kill a person for money than for your beliefs. Because everyone values money the same, but your opinions, however strong and sacred, are only valuable to you."
