title = "Fallacy of Human Malleability"
summary = "Why the policies we make for equity and equality is doomed to fail, yet perpetually exist"
+++

This notion that because there is some, there must be more, and that because a little is good, more must be better is falsely transitive. And this is the problem we face with our perception of human malleability and how we frame and make policy decisions.

Think about how JFK's administration basically had a room full of Keynsians for economic advisors, and how they believed in infinitely manipulative Taylor curves, thereby ignoring the Philips curve and leading the country to the stagflation of the 1970s. There is absolutely malleability in human, but the fact is, we don't know if this is externally influencible or exploitable in any way, and even if it is and there are ways, there are inherent limitations to the human malleability. But we make wrongful intuitive leap in logic that because we see evidence of some, there must be more - that because a little is good, more must be better.
